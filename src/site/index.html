01 · the gates

Four gates. A specification clears all of them or it is logged with the reason it died.

The sweep does not rank. It kills. Each gate below took the count that reached it and passed on what survived. The kill reasons are printed beside the counts because a reason is worth more than a rank.

Gate 01 · point in time

48 → 31

17 killed. Restated fundamentals had leaked inside the fit window.

Every dataset is rebuilt as it was reported on each historical date, with the restatement lag and the index membership of the day. Anything that knew something it could not have known is stopped here.

Gate 02 · walk forward

31 → 31

0 killed. Thirty one in, thirty one out.

Fitted on one window, tested on the next, rolled across several regimes. In this run every specification that reached this gate held its out of sample Sharpe above the floor. A gate that killed nothing is still a gate, and the log says so.

Gate 03 · costs and slippage

31 → 14

17 killed. Edge under 4 bps against a 7 bp round trip.

Spread, commission and impact are charged on every fill before anyone sees a curve. Seventeen ideas that looked fine trading free could not pay for their own turnover.

Gate 04 · capacity

14 → 6

8 killed. Under 40 million dollars before impact ate the edge.

The size at which a strategy’s own footprint removes what it earns, stated in dollars before allocation. Eight survivors of the first three gates were too small to be worth a meeting.

6 / 48. The denominator stays on the screen so the numerator can be priced.

02 · the deliverable

Five things a backtesting library does not hand you

A library will run your loop. It will not tell you how many loops you ran, what each fill cost, or how much money the idea can carry. Those five columns are the product.
